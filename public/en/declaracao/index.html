Who files

Who needs to file?

It depends on how you earn money in Ireland.

I

Employee (PAYE)

Tax is already taken from your salary. Even so, it's worth filing the annual return in myAccount: that's where you add the credits and expenses that were left out and get back what you overpaid. You can review the last 4 years.

If you have other income outside your job (rent, freelance work, interest): up to €5,000 net a year, and under €30,000 gross, it goes in your myAccount return. Above that, you file as a self-employed person.

II

Self-employed

If you work for yourself, you must file a return every year (Form 11, through ROS, Revenue's online system).

Along with it, you pay the balance for the previous year and pay part of the current year in advance ("preliminary tax").

Deadlines

The dates that matter.

  1. 31 October

    Standard self-employed deadline

    File the previous year's return and pay the balance, plus preliminary tax for the current year.

  2. Mid-November

    Extended ROS deadline

    If you both file and pay online through ROS, you get a few extra days. In 2026 the deadline is 18 November. The date changes every year.

  3. 31 December

    Last day to claim back the oldest year

    Refunds cover the last 4 years. In 2026, it's the last day to claim for 2022.

  4. From January

    Employee (PAYE) return

    The return for the year just ended becomes available in myAccount, and you can claim back what you overpaid.

Before you start

What to get ready.

You don't need everything to get in touch: I'll tell you what's missing for your case.

  • Access to myAccount or ROS, Revenue's online system. Keep your PPSN and passwords to yourself: don't send them through the form.
  • A summary of what you earned in the year: the Employment Detail Summary in myAccount, or your payslips.
  • Receipts for expenses that may give you a credit, such as health, rent and courses.
  • If you're self-employed: a record of your business income and expenses, with invoices and statements.
  • Other income: rent, interest, investments and income abroad, including in Brazil. They may need to go in your return.
